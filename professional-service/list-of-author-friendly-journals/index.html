List of Author-Friendly Journals

Essential Advice for Novice Researchers

If you are a first-time author looking to publish in one of these journals, keep the following strategies in mind:

  1. Match Your Paper to the Indexing Pressure:

    • If you need a prestigious, indexed publication (for graduation or promotion), target AJTE, IJLTER, or JSSER. However, be prepared for strict desk rejections if your methodology isn't flawless.

    • If you are just starting out and want constructive feedback without the intense pressure, ESBB or The New English Teacher are much more nurturing environments.

  2. Watch out for APCs (Article Processing Charges):

    • Journals like JTL (University of Windsor) and ESBB are free to publish in.

    • Open-access journals indexed in Scopus (like IJLTER) usually charge fees that can range from a few hundred to over a thousand dollars. Always verify the exact fee on the journal’s "Author Guidelines" or "About" page before submitting.

  3. Strict Adherence to Guidelines:

    • Novices frequently get rejected simply for formatting errors. If a journal like IJLTER says "minimum 5500 words, strictly APA 7th," a 4,500-word submission in APA 6th will be immediately desk-rejected without peer review.

  4. The Cover Letter Matters:

    • For journals like Professions and Professionalism, you cannot just upload your paper. You must write a compelling cover letter explaining exactly how your research advances their specific field of study.

  5. English Language Quality:

    • Almost all of these journals warn that poor English will result in immediate rejection. If English is not your first language, utilize a professional academic proofreader before clicking submit.
